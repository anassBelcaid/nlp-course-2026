Project 02 · The Embedding Laboratory

Inspect, train, and compare word representations

Explore pretrained vectors, build Word2Vec step by step in PyTorch, and complete a guided GloVe implementation.

Duration: Two weeks
Mode: Individual
Format: One guided Jupyter notebook
Deliverable: Completed notebook with outputs and a 300–450 word reflection

The idea

You will study word embeddings at three increasing levels of difficulty:

  1. Explore: load published GloVe vectors, solve analogies, and visualize semantic groups.
  2. Build: train Skip-gram Word2Vec step by step with PyTorch on a manageable corpus slice.
  3. Transfer: reuse the same corpus to implement the three essential pieces of GloVe.

The notebook provides data preparation, batching, training infrastructure, plotting, evaluation helpers, and immediate checks. Every blank includes layered, foldable hints so that you can recover from being stuck without being handed the answer immediately.

What you will implement

Part I · Pretrained embeddings

Exactly three tasks:

  • load glove-wiki-gigaword-50 with Gensim;
  • solve vector analogies with positive and negative terms;
  • create a two-dimensional PCA projection for supplied plotting code.

Part II · Word2Vec with PyTorch

Build the learning pipeline one idea at a time:

  • generate positive Skip-gram center–context pairs;
  • sample negative contexts from a smoothed unigram distribution;
  • implement two embedding tables and their dot-product score;
  • complete a standard PyTorch optimization step;
  • inspect nearest neighbors and visualize the learned weight matrix.

Part III · GloVe

The model, dataset, optimizer, training loop, evaluation, and visualization are supplied. You complete exactly three essential blanks:

  1. accumulate inverse-distance co-occurrence counts;
  2. implement the GloVe weighting function;
  3. predict log counts and return weighted squared error.

Runtime and corpus

The notebook uses the first 40,000 tokens of text8, caps the vocabulary at 2,500 words, and trains in large batches. These defaults prioritize a smooth Colab CPU experience. The learned neighborhoods will be noisier than published vectors—that contrast is part of the analysis.

Note

The first run downloads text8 and the 50-dimensional GloVe model. Later runs reuse the Gensim cache. A GPU is optional.

Submission

Submit embedding_lab.ipynb with:

  • all ten TODO checks passing;
  • all training curves, tables, and plots visible;
  • responses to the short interpretation prompts; and
  • a 300–450 word final reflection.

Before submitting, restart the kernel and run the entire notebook from top to bottom.
