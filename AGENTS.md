# NLP Course Authoring Guide

## Incremental review

- When the instructor asks to develop a lecture section by section, create only the requested section and stop for review before continuing.

## Teaching sequence

- Introduce each abstraction through one small, concrete example whose values remain visible while the formal rule is derived.
- Make intermediate representations inspectable: show the tokens, counts, vectors, tables, scores, or errors that produce the result.
- For a first calculation, state the givens and exact task in a conspicuous `PAUSE` panel, then place the worked solution on the following slide rather than revealing it on the prompt slide.
- End each part with a compact synthesis and one question that creates the need for the next part.

## Slide space and legibility

- Vary the form of the scientific argument: use diagrams for structure, controlled demonstrations for mechanisms, equations for precise rules, and published figures or benchmark tables for evidence. Approval of one component is not a request to repeat it on every slide. Keep a consistent visual identity without imposing one layout or interaction pattern on the lecture.
- Choose research evidence when it serves the question, not to fill a media checklist. Label illustrative numbers and measured outputs clearly, cite primary sources, and state the evaluation setting for benchmark comparisons.

- Use the available slide canvas deliberately. Content should normally occupy most of the usable height instead of clustering in the upper three quarters and leaving a large inactive lower area.
- Improve space usage primarily by increasing the size and visibility of meaningful content: type, equations, matrices, examples, diagrams, and spacing between related elements.
- Give cards and boxes deliberate dimensions, then keep their text at a readable presentation size and allow it to wrap naturally across as many lines as needed. Never shrink box text or force it onto one line merely to preserve a horizontal layout.
- When several boxes make the text too small, use fewer columns, taller boxes, or a second row. Treat multi-line text as the normal case and verify that labels, explanations, and values wrap without clipping or overflow.
- Do not fill space with unnecessary prose, decorative objects, or cumbersome layouts. A slide may retain breathing room, but the composition should feel intentionally balanced across the full canvas.
- After an instructor review identifies this issue, apply the correction consistently to earlier slides in the same presentation as well as to newly authored slides.

## Lecture overview images

- Every lecture presentation should include an original, purpose-made overview image near the beginning that previews the lecture's complete conceptual journey.
- After the title slide, open with the visual overview. Do not begin a lecture with a text-heavy question slide.
- The overview image must teach or orient: it should reveal the major stages and their relationships rather than serve as decoration.
- Keep generated images free of embedded text when practical; add precise labels in the slide source so wording remains editable and accessible.
- Store the final image locally beside the lecture and provide useful alt text.

## Lecture endings and visual bridges

- End each lecture with a purposeful visual bridge to the next lecture, preferably a large, memorable diagram or animated flow rather than a text-only closing slide.
- Preserve intellectual suspense: establish the limitation or unresolved question that motivates the next lecture, but do not prematurely teach the next lecture's equations or full solution.
- Make the final visual expose the structure of the upcoming idea while leaving its central mechanism open for discussion.
- When the presentation technology supports animation, use motion to reveal information flow, state changes, dependencies, or another conceptually meaningful transition; do not animate merely for decoration.
- Finish with one concise question that students should carry into the next lecture.
